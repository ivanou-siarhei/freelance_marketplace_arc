## Implementation Plan: ArcWork (Freelance Job Marketplace)

### Summary
A Freelancer-style marketplace where clients post jobs, freelancers bid, and the agreed amount is locked in a USDC escrow contract on Arc Testnet until the work is approved, auto-released after a review timeout, or settled by an arbiter. Job listings, bids, profiles and deliverables live in Postgres behind a small backend. Testnet only.

Delivery is split in two milestones. Milestone 1 builds the complete contract plus the core job-to-payout flow, then stops for your review. Milestone 2 (dispute UI, arbiter panel, polish) starts only after you approve Milestone 1.

### Architecture
- Blockchain: Arc Testnet (chain ID 5042002). USDC is the gas token and the payment token (ERC-20 view, 6 decimals, 0x3600000000000000000000000000000000000000). Read chain and USDC facts from `@/onchain-facts` and format amounts with `@/onchain-money`.
- Contract: `contracts/JobEscrow.sol` (Solidity 0.8.28, OpenZeppelin `SafeERC20`, `ReentrancyGuard`). The build confirms the pinned OpenZeppelin 5.6.1 is installed and only falls back to the latest compatible 5.x if it is not. One contract holds all escrows. The COMPLETE contract, including the dispute functions, is written, reviewed, tested and deployed in Milestone 1 (it is not upgradeable, so it is deployed once). Only the dispute UI and arbiter panel wait for Milestone 2.
  - Arbiter: a separate third wallet that you provide. Its address is a constructor argument, requested before deploy. Testing uses three wallets: client, freelancer, arbiter.
  - Constructor timing parameters (immutable, with enforced floors so a bad value cannot be deployed): `arbiterTimeout` (floor 5 minutes, cap 30 days), `minDeliveryPeriod` (floor 1 minute), and `minReviewPeriod` (floor 1 minute), with a fixed `maxReviewPeriod` and a maximum delivery window. I added `minReviewPeriod` alongside the two you named, because demoing the review timeout needs it too. Two deploy profiles are offered when we get to deployment: a "demo" profile (for example arbiterTimeout 10 minutes, minDeliveryPeriod 5 minutes, minReviewPeriod 2 minutes) and a "realistic" profile (arbiterTimeout 14 days, minDeliveryPeriod 1 day, minReviewPeriod 1 day). The contract is not upgradeable, so changing profile means redeploying. The backend and UI read these values from contract getters instead of hardcoding them.
  - Time boundaries (all comparisons are exact and exclusive on one side only, so there is no overlap and no gap): `reviewEndsAt = submittedAt + reviewPeriod`. `dispute` requires `block.timestamp < reviewEndsAt`. `claimAfterTimeout` requires `block.timestamp >= reviewEndsAt`. `cancel` requires `block.timestamp > deliveryDeadline`. `resolveStale` requires `block.timestamp >= disputedAt + arbiterTimeout`. `approve` and `resolve` have no time limit while their state allows them.
  - `createEscrow(bytes32 jobRef, address freelancer, uint256 amount, uint64 deliveryDeadline, uint64 reviewPeriod)`: called by the client after accepting a bid. Reverts on zero addresses, `client == freelancer`, arbiter equal to client or freelancer, a `(client, jobRef)` pair that was already funded, a `deliveryDeadline` earlier than `block.timestamp + minDeliveryPeriod` or beyond a maximum (for example 90 days), or a `reviewPeriod` outside the min/max bounds. The uniqueness guard is keyed on the pair, not on `jobRef` alone, so a third party can never front-run a client by funding the same `jobRef` first; their escrow would sit under their own address and the real client's funding still succeeds. A public view `escrowIdByRef(client, jobRef)` lets the backend and UI see whether a pair is already funded. Pulls USDC with `transferFrom`. Emits `EscrowCreated(escrowId, jobRef, client, freelancer, amount, deliveryDeadline, reviewPeriod)`.
  - `submitWork(escrowId, bytes32 deliveryHash)`: freelancer only. Allowed any time until the client has cancelled, so late delivery is possible but the client keeps the right to cancel after the deadline. Stores the hash and starts the review window. Emits `WorkSubmitted(escrowId, deliveryHash, reviewEndsAt)`.
  - `approve(escrowId)`: client only. Credits the freelancer's claimable balance.
  - `cancel(escrowId)`: client only, only if no work was submitted AND `deliveryDeadline` has passed. The client cannot cancel while the freelancer is working. Credits the client's claimable balance.
  - `decline(escrowId)`: freelancer only, only before work is submitted. Credits the client's claimable balance (the early-exit path with the freelancer's consent).
  - `claimAfterTimeout(escrowId)`: anyone, only when `block.timestamp >= reviewEndsAt` with no approval and no dispute. Credits the freelancer (auto-release).
  - `dispute(escrowId, bytes32 reasonHash)`: client only, only while `block.timestamp < reviewEndsAt`. Freezes the escrow for the arbiter. The reason text and evidence stay offchain in the database; the hash makes them tamper-evident.
  - `resolve(escrowId, uint256 freelancerAmount)`: arbiter only, on disputed escrows, `freelancerAmount <= amount`. Credits the freelancer and the client with the remainder. Reverts if the current arbiter is a party to that escrow.
  - `resolveStale(escrowId)`: anyone, once a dispute has sat unresolved for `arbiterTimeout` (set at deploy; 14 days in the realistic profile). Because work was submitted before any dispute can exist, the default is 100% to the freelancer, so stalling the arbiter never benefits the client. The arbiter may still call `resolve` until someone calls `resolveStale`. No dispute deposit in v1.
  - `withdraw()`: pull payments. Every payout (approve, cancel, decline, timeout, resolve, stale) credits `claimable[address]`, and the caller withdraws their own balance in USDC. A blocklisted or reverting recipient can never block the other party's payout.
  - `setArbiter(address)`: current arbiter only; the new arbiter cannot be zero.
  - Approval flow: default is `approve` then `createEscrow` (two transactions). Before building, the build probes the Arc USDC contract onchain for EIP-2612 `permit` (public sources conflict on whether it is supported). If it is present, `createEscrow` gets a permit variant; otherwise the two-step flow stays.
  - State machine: Funded -> Submitted -> (Released | Disputed -> Resolved) and Funded -> Cancelled (via client cancel after deadline, or freelancer decline). No platform fee in v1. Custom errors, events for every transition, checks-effects-interactions.
- Backend: small Bun HTTP server in `server/` talking to Postgres. Postgres runs as a Docker container (`postgres:18-alpine`, named volume mounted at `/var/lib/postgresql` as required by the PG18 image, not `/var/lib/postgresql/data`; published on 127.0.0.1:5432) with `DATABASE_URL` in `.env`. The build checks that Docker works first; only if it does not, it falls back to Bun's built-in SQLite (WAL mode, a single process, never silently rebuilt on corruption) and the setup steps are adjusted. Vite proxies `/api` to the server.
  - Auth: Sign-In with Ethereum (EIP-4361) using `viem/siwe`. `POST /api/auth/nonce` issues a single-use nonce with a short expiry. `POST /api/auth/verify` checks the signed message: `domain` against a server-side allowlist only, `SIWE_ALLOWED_DOMAINS` in `.env`. The allowlist is never built from the `Origin` or `Host` headers of the incoming request, because a phishing site would then pass its own domain check. Entries are either exact hosts (for this session: `i3i07ew7q56om721roffa.preview.studio.arc.io`) or an explicit parent-domain rule written `*.preview.studio.arc.io`, which matches any subdomain under that suffix at a label boundary and never the bare suffix itself. The wildcard exists so preview hosts that change between sessions keep working; its trade-off is that it trusts every host under that parent, so the exact host is listed first and the wildcard is kept only because this is testnet. The client builds the message domain from the page it is running on; the server decides whether to accept it. The `uri` field is checked against the same allowlist, `chainId` 5042002, the nonce (consumed on use), and the time window. It returns a session token with a TTL (stored hashed). The token goes in the `Authorization` header, never in a URL. Write endpoints require a session and check the caller owns the record.
  - Tables (Milestone 1): `users` (address, display_name, bio, skills), `jobs` (id, client_address, title, description, category, budget_min, budget_max, deadline, review_period_seconds, status, accepted_bid_id, accepted_at, funding_expires_at, job_ref), `bids` (id, job_id, freelancer_address, amount, delivery_seconds, proposal, status: open / accepted / funded / withdrawn / rejected), `deliveries` (job_id, note, link, delivery_hash, submitted_at), `escrows` (job_id, escrow_id, job_ref, amount, freelancer, review_ends_at, onchain_status, last_block), `indexer_anomalies` (tx_hash, log_index, job_ref, reason, raw_event, seen_at), `sessions`, `nonces`.
  - Table (Milestone 2): `disputes` (job_id, reason, evidence_link, reason_hash, opened_at, freelancer_response, responded_at) so the arbiter has both sides' material. The reason is stored offchain; only the dispute event and its hash go onchain.
  - Job status: open -> in_escrow -> submitted -> completed, with cancelled and disputed branches.
  - Delivery hash: `delivery_hash` is the keccak256 of a canonical record of the delivery note and link. The server recomputes it, and the UI shows whether the stored record matches the onchain `deliveryHash`. A hash of a link does not pin what the link points to later, so the UI says so and recommends content-addressed or immutable links. File uploads are out of scope.
  - Chain sync: an indexer loop in the same server process polls `JobEscrow` events (filtered to the configured contract address) with viem `getLogs` and updates `escrows` and `jobs`. `POST /api/jobs/:id/sync` verifies a tx hash on demand so the UI updates right after a transaction.
  - `job_ref` is derived, not chosen freely: when a bid is accepted the server computes `keccak256(abi.encode(jobId, bidId, client, freelancer, amount))` and stores it on the job. The contract cannot verify the derivation, so the indexer does. Because it binds the client and the accepted bid, a stale or foreign reference never matches.
  - `EscrowCreated` is cross-checked against the database before a job becomes `in_escrow`. All of these must hold: `jobRef` equals the stored `job_ref` of an existing job; `client` equals `jobs.client_address`; `freelancer` and `amount` equal the accepted bid; the bid is still in `accepted` status and the job has no verified escrow yet; `deliveryDeadline >= event block timestamp + delivery_seconds` of the accepted bid; and `reviewPeriod` equals the job's `review_period_seconds` (which the freelancer sees before bidding, and which was itself validated against the contract's min/max bounds). I read "within agreed limits" as exactly the agreed value; tell me if you want a range instead. Any failure is written to `indexer_anomalies` with the reason, logged, and the job is NOT marked `in_escrow`. The UI adds a buffer (for example one hour) to the deadline it sends, so mining delay cannot make an honest client's deadline fail the check. The database never marks money as moved without a matching, verified onchain event.
  - Accepted-but-unfunded bids: once a bid is `accepted`, the freelancer can no longer edit or withdraw it, and the client cannot accept a second bid on that job. The client gets a "Cancel acceptance" action (`POST /api/jobs/:id/unaccept`) that returns the bid and the job to `open`. The server refuses it if the contract's `escrowIdByRef` shows the pair is already funded. Acceptance also expires on its own at `funding_expires_at` (for example 48 hours after acceptance), so a freelancer is never held indefinitely when funding never arrives. If a funding transaction was already in flight when acceptance was cancelled or expired, the resulting event does not match an accepted bid, is logged as an anomaly, and the Dashboard shows the client an "unmatched escrow" notice explaining that the funds stay recoverable through the freelancer's decline or the client's cancel after the deadline.
  - Delivery time is stored in seconds (`delivery_seconds`) instead of whole days, because the demo deployment profile uses minutes. The UI shows days and hours normally and offers short "demo" durations only when the deployed contract reports a short `minDeliveryPeriod`.
  - Endpoints: jobs (list, filter, create, cancel while open), bids (create, list, withdraw, accept), deliveries (submit), profiles (get, upsert), escrow state, health. Milestone 2 adds dispute create/respond/read.
- Frontend: React 18 with standard SCSS instead of Tailwind (`sass` added, SCSS modules plus shared tokens/mixins partials; the Tailwind directives, config and packages are removed). `react-router-dom` for pages, TanStack Query for API data, wagmi/viem for contract reads and writes, Sonner toasts. Keep the Arc/Circle visual direction, expressed in SCSS. Pages:
  - Browse jobs (search, category filter, budget range). (M1)
  - Job detail (M1): client sees bids, an "Accept and fund" action (USDC approve, then `createEscrow`), and a "Cancel acceptance" action plus a funding countdown while a bid is accepted but unfunded; freelancer sees a bid form, "Decline" before submitting, a "Submit work" action, and a review countdown; client sees Approve, and Cancel once the deadline has passed with no submission; anyone sees "Claim after timeout" once it is available. The "Dispute" button is added in M2.
  - Post a job. (M1)
  - Dashboard (M1): my posted jobs, my bids, my active escrows, and an Earnings card showing the claimable balance with a Withdraw button.
  - Profile: view and edit. (M1)
  - Arbiter panel (M2): shown only when the connected wallet is the arbiter; lists disputes with reason, evidence and the freelancer's response, resolves with a slider for the freelancer's share, and offers the stale-dispute action.
- Wallet: ConnectKit with the injected connector (existing `src/config.ts`), Arc Testnet as the active chain, plus a sign-in step that creates the backend session. Faucet link (https://faucet.circle.com) when the balance is zero.

### Build Sequence
**Milestone 1: complete contract and core flow**
1. Ask for the arbiter wallet address (a third wallet, distinct from the test client and freelancer) and which timing profile to deploy with (demo or realistic). Probe whether Arc USDC supports `permit`. Write the complete escrow contract, run a balanced review, run unit, invariant and fuzz tests, verify it on a fork of the chain, then deploy to Arc Testnet. Store the address in `.env` as `VITE_ESCROW_ADDRESS` and keep the ABI from the compiled artifact.
2. Check Docker, start Postgres with a named volume, wait until it is ready, and set `DATABASE_URL` (SQLite fallback only if Docker is unavailable).
3. Build the backend: schema migration, SIWE auth with session TTL, jobs / bids / profiles / escrows routes, and the event indexer with the `EscrowCreated` cross-checks and anomaly log. Add the Vite `/api` proxy and the `dev:server` script, then start the server.
4. Swap Tailwind for SCSS (tokens, mixins, global styles), then build the frontend foundation: router, layout, API client, session hook, wallet connect and SIWE sign-in.
5. Build Browse jobs, Post job, and Profile against the live API.
6. Build Job detail: bidding, bid acceptance, USDC approve + `createEscrow`, then post-funding sync to the database.
7. Build the delivery flow: submit work with `deliveryHash`, review countdown, approve, decline, cancel after deadline, claim-after-timeout, and the Dashboard Earnings card with Withdraw.
8. Run typecheck and lint, then walk through the core flow with the client and freelancer wallets: post, bid, accept and fund, submit, approve, withdraw; then timeout claim, decline, and cancel after deadline.
9. STOP. Summarize what was built and what was verified, and wait for your review. Milestone 2 does not start until you approve.

**Milestone 2: disputes and polish (only after your approval)**
10. Create the `disputes` table and routes; build the Dispute form (reason, evidence link, onchain `dispute` with `reasonHash`), the freelancer response, and dispute status on Job detail.
11. Build the Arbiter panel (resolve with a split, stale-dispute action) and walk through dispute, resolve, and stale cases with all three wallets.
12. Polish: loading, empty and error states, zero-balance faucet hint, explorer links for transactions, mobile layout, consistent USDC formatting. Re-run typecheck and lint.

### Done When
**Milestone 1**
- [ ] The complete `JobEscrow` (including dispute, resolve and resolveStale) is deployed to Arc Testnet with your arbiter wallet; unit, invariant and fuzz tests pass.
- [ ] The cancel rule holds: the client cannot cancel while the freelancer is working (only after the deadline with no submission, or after the freelancer declines).
- [ ] The contract rejects client == freelancer and an arbiter who is the client or the freelancer.
- [ ] Funding uniqueness is per `(client, jobRef)`: a third party funding the same `jobRef` cannot block the real client, and funding the same pair twice reverts.
- [ ] `arbiterTimeout`, `minDeliveryPeriod` and `minReviewPeriod` are constructor arguments with enforced floors; the chosen profile is deployed and the app reads the values from the contract.
- [ ] Time boundaries behave exactly as specified (`dispute` only before `reviewEndsAt`, `claimAfterTimeout` from `reviewEndsAt`, `cancel` only after `deliveryDeadline`, `resolveStale` from `disputedAt + arbiterTimeout`), with tests at both sides of each boundary and long intervals checked via `vm.warp`.
- [ ] An `EscrowCreated` event with a wrong `jobRef`, client, freelancer, amount, a deadline shorter than the accepted bid's delivery time, or a `reviewPeriod` different from the job's setting is logged in `indexer_anomalies` and leaves the job out of `in_escrow`.
- [ ] An accepted bid cannot be edited or withdrawn by the freelancer; the client can cancel the acceptance (blocked if already funded), and unfunded acceptances expire so the job returns to open.
- [ ] SIWE accepts only domains in the server-side `SIWE_ALLOWED_DOMAINS` allowlist (exact hosts and the explicit parent-domain rule) and ignores `Origin` and `Host` headers for that decision.
- [ ] All payouts are claimable balances; the Dashboard shows them and Withdraw sends USDC to the caller.
- [ ] Postgres (or the SQLite fallback) persists data and the backend creates its schema on its own.
- [ ] A user can connect a wallet, sign in with SIWE (domain, chainId, single-use nonce, session TTL enforced), and edit a profile.
- [ ] A client can post a job; freelancers can place, edit and withdraw bids; only the client can accept one.
- [ ] Accepting a bid funds escrow with USDC, and the job moves to "in escrow" only after a verified `EscrowCreated` event; a mismatched event is logged in `indexer_anomalies` and does not change the job.
- [ ] The freelancer can submit work with a `deliveryHash` that the UI can compare to the stored record; the client can approve; the freelancer withdraws the USDC.
- [ ] If the client does nothing, the freelancer can claim funds after the review timeout.
- [ ] Non-owners cannot change other users' jobs, bids or profiles through the API.
- [ ] The UI uses SCSS with no Tailwind left, the app is Arc Testnet only, no secrets are in client code, and typecheck and lint pass.
- [ ] Work stops here for your review.

**Milestone 2**
- [ ] The client can dispute inside the review window with a reason and evidence link (stored in `disputes`, hashed onchain); the freelancer can respond.
- [ ] The arbiter wallet can resolve a dispute with any split; a dispute left unresolved past the arbiter timeout can be settled and pays the freelancer in full.
- [ ] Polish items are done and typecheck and lint pass.
